Assumptions and open questions

No car parking

A ground-floor bedroom and a living room use up the full 20′ width. The 3′ front setback only fits two-wheelers. If you need a car, the usual fix is stilt parking with the duplex on floors 1 and 2, or you give up Bedroom 1.

Check local bylaws

This plan assumes a 3′ front setback, 2′ rear setback and no side setbacks (shared walls). That gives about 83% ground coverage. Many authorities cap coverage at 60–75% and require a side setback on plots this narrow. Confirm with your BBMP, GHMC, BDA or local authority before drawing working plans.

Light and air

Both side walls are party walls, so windows are only on the south and north faces. Bath 1 is in the middle of the house and needs a mechanical exhaust ducted to the rear. A 3′ × 4′ skylight over the ground-floor passage would help the middle of the house a lot.

Glass on the south

The concrete frame projects 3′ in front of the glass, and vertical wood fins cover the upper panel. Together they block the high summer sun and let the low winter sun in. Use double-glazed, low-E glass, with motorised blinds on the upper tier.

Double-height living

The ceiling is about 19½′ high. Use a ceiling fan on a long downrod or a large-blade fan, and add a pendant light on a 7′ drop. The gallery's glass rail should be 3′6″ high in toughened laminated glass.

Stair

Dog-leg stair, 3′6″ flights, 16 risers of 7½″ and treads of 11″, with a 3′6″ landing on the west wall. It continues up to the terrace.

Flex room

The first-floor study (10′ × 14′) sits over the kitchen and has a north window. It can become Bedroom 3 later with a wardrobe wall. The design has no pooja room, so this room can also hold one.
